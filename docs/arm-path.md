# Arm path: biceps exercises from two EMG sensors

A third mode next to the Arcade and the head-and-face Practice path. People practise with
their biceps instead of their face. The only input is two yes/no signals: **is the left
biceps flexing?** and **is the right biceps flexing?** There is no camera.

## What the research says, and what it means here

- **Biofeedback helps people with CP, but the evidence is thin.** A systematic review of 57
  studies found most reported gains, but rated the evidence "positive, very low" because
  most studies were small and uncontrolled ([MacIntosh 2019][r1]). A month of wearable EMG
  biofeedback on the arm improved goals in all 10 children who finished a pilot study
  ([Bloom 2010][r2]). *So: frame it as practice and motivation, not treatment.*
- **In CP the biceps are often over-active, not weak.** EMG biofeedback studies usually aim
  to *lower* overactive biceps and raise the triceps; adding a game improved the
  triceps-to-biceps balance and elbow extension ([EMG + VR study][r3]). *So: learning to
  switch the biceps **off** on cue (Let go, Stay loose) is as important as switching it on.
  Rewards are never "flex harder".*
- **Strength training for CP follows normal youth guidelines:** start with 1–2 sets of 8–15
  easy reps to learn the technique, build to about 3 sets of 10, rest at least a minute
  between sets, 2+ non-consecutive days a week ([Verschuren 2016][r4], [Scholtes 2008][r5]).
  *So: Curls lessons are 10 reps at a steady tempo; driving between gates is the rest; the
  app suggests arm days every other day.*
- **Two-sensor (dual-site) muscle control is trained in exactly these steps** for people
  learning myoelectric prostheses: switch each muscle on and off alone, hold it, then
  sequence, then both together (co-contraction) as a separate signal
  ([ADAPT-MP games][r6], [UNB control metrics][r7]). Serious games trained this as well as
  coaching in small studies ([JNER 2021][r8]). *So: the unit order is on/off → hold →
  rhythm → left vs right vs both.*
- **Mirror movements** (the other arm joins in) are common in one-sided CP. Children could
  suppress them when cued, but a pilot found coping strategies may help more than drilling
  suppression ([mirror movement studies][r9]). *So: "Just one" starts by allowing the
  other arm to join for a while and only slowly asks for it to stay quiet. A miss never
  blocks progress.*

## What a yes/no signal can measure

| Measure | How | Exercise it trains |
|---|---|---|
| Turn on when asked | flex seen after the cue | activation |
| Turn off when asked | relax seen after the cue | relaxation, spasticity control |
| Reaction time | cue → first flex | speed of starting a movement |
| Hold time | seconds kept on | endurance (isometric) |
| Stay relaxed | seconds kept off | not over-activating |
| Timing | on/off within a window of a beat | rhythm, curl tempo |
| Which side | the named arm on, the other off | selective control |
| Together | both on within a short window | bilateral coordination |

It can't measure how hard or how far. Nothing in the path asks for "more".

## The roadmap: 4 units, 5 lessons each, then a boss race on the real car

Same shape as the head path: every lesson is a course with 10 gates, one rep per gate, the
drive between gates is the rest, a miss still opens the gate, difficulty adapts per lesson
(≥ 9/10 → harder, < 6/10 → easier), gold after two good days.

**Unit 1 · Wake up** — find each biceps and switch it on and off on cue.
1. **Squeeze**: squeeze the named arm and keep it on for a moment (0.5 s → 2 s).
2. **Let go**: squeeze, then on "Let go!" relax fully in time and stay relaxed (3 s → 1 s to relax).
3. **Hold**: keep a squeeze on (2 s → 6 s). Letting go pauses the clock, it doesn't reset it.
4. **Quick**: wait for the cue, then squeeze as fast as you can (2.5 s → 0.8 s). Squeezing early just restarts the wait.
5. **Mix**: all of the above in random order.
- Boss **Flex Sprint**: squeeze either arm to drive; the car steers itself.

**Unit 2 · Hold strong** — endurance and controlled release.
1. **Long hold**: 3 s → 12 s.
2. **Hold, then let go**: hold, then relax on cue within 1.5 s.
3. **Both arms hold**: squeeze both and hold (2 s → 8 s).
4. **Stay loose**: keep both arms relaxed (3 s → 10 s). For over-active biceps.
5. **Mix** (+ review reps from Unit 1).
- Boss **Long Haul**: the car only moves while you hold a squeeze, and every 3 s held gives a boost.

**Unit 3 · Curls** — a set of curls at a steady tempo: up, hold at the top, down.
1. **Up and down**: "Up!" squeeze within the window and keep it 2 s, "Down" relax within the window and stay down 2 s (window 1.2 s → 0.5 s).
2. **Pause at the top**: the same with a longer top hold (2 s → 5 s).
3. **Both arms**: curls with both arms together.
4. **Fast up**: squeeze as fast as you can on "Up!", hold 1 s, relax.
5. **Mix** (+ review).
- Boss **Pump Track**: the car rolls by itself; every new squeeze (let go, squeeze again) gives a boost. Holding does nothing, so it's a set of curls.

**Unit 4 · Left and right** — use one arm without the other, switch, and work together.
1. **Just one**: squeeze the named arm for 1 s while the other stays relaxed. The other arm may join for 1 s at first, then less, then not at all.
2. **Switch**: squeeze one arm, let go, squeeze the other (6 s → 2.5 s for the switch).
3. **Together**: squeeze both at the same moment (within 0.8 s → 0.25 s) and hold 1 s.
4. **Which arm?**: a random arm is named; squeeze that one fast and not the other.
5. **Mix** (+ review).
- Boss **Arm Grand Prix** on Pine Speedway (long straights, gentle 90° corners, so the fast real car has room): you steer. Left arm turns right, right arm turns left, both drive straight at full speed, neither slows down. The real car follows.

Safety, in the app: a short warm-up rep starts each lesson; the "I'm tired" button ends a
lesson and still counts; two fatigue signs (slower reactions, more misses, sensor dropping
out) suggest a rest; the app says to practise seated, to stop if anything hurts, and to add
a weight only if a therapist says so.

## Hardware and data flow

```
biceps → EMG sensor (e.g. MyoWare, envelope output) → ESP32 → wss://…/api/emg?token=CAR_TOKEN → game server → phone/laptop
```

- The ESP32 sends `f <left> <right>` (each 0 or 1) when either changes, and every 0.5 s
  as a heartbeat. No message for 2 s = sensor offline (lessons pause, the car waits).
- `firmware/emg_arms/emg_arms.ino`: reads two analog envelope pins (or two digital
  flex pins if your board already decides), auto-calibrates the relaxed level at power-up,
  thresholds with hysteresis, and sends the line above.
- The car's own ESP32 may send the same `f` lines instead, if one board does both.
- One sensor rig: everyone in the Arm path sees the same arms (fine for a demo).
- No sensor? Hold the **A / D** (or ← / →) keys, or hold the on-screen Left/Right buttons.

## Where it lives

- `/arms` (third card on the first screen), name screen, then a **sensor check** screen:
  both arms light up live, with steps "Squeeze left ✓ / Squeeze right ✓ / Relax both ✓",
  a "Swap left and right" switch and "Use keys or touch instead".
- `public/path.js`: the 4 arm units (ids `a1`–`a4`) with their own lessons and knobs, in the
  same `UNITS` list with `path: 'arms'`; progress shares the person's XP, streak and goal.
- `public/arms.js`: building arm lessons and `ArmRun`, the rep engine for yes/no signals
  (same interface as `LessonRun`, so the lesson courses, HUD and scoring are reused).
- `server/arena.js`: an `emg` device role; players in the Arm path subscribe to it.

## The real car (MuscleCar over Bluetooth)

- The game page connects to the MuscleCar ESP32-S3 with Web Bluetooth (`public/musclecar.js`),
  reads both biceps (about 50 readings a second), and decides flexing with thresholds from a
  6-second calibration (relax, squeeze left, squeeze right), like Free Flex's switch.
- Mapping (as in Free Flex): each arm drives its own side's wheels. Left arm = turn right, right
  arm = turn left, both = straight.
- Both-arms window: one arm alone only counts as a turn after 250 ms alone; both and none count at
  once. So a slightly late second arm still goes straight, and an uneven let-go doesn't swerve.
- The Bluetooth car uses binary wheel buttons during local races, lessons and demo mode. Each calibrated bicep holds its forward wheel button while flexed. In Arm Grand Prix and demo, a two-arm squeeze holds both buttons through a one-arm dropout. Relax both before starting a new turn. Eight seconds after straight drive starts, it stops and requires both arms to rest. Losing the sensor stream also requires a fresh rest. Game speed and motor percentages do not gate these commands.
- Lessons: between gates the car only moves while the arms give the direction the road needs.
