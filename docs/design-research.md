## Built on real research

These studies informed the practice design.

- **Relaxing counts as much as squeezing.** In CP the biceps are often over-active, so letting go on cue is a skill ([EMG + VR study](https://content.iospress.com/articles/neurorehabilitation/nre1402)). The game never says "squeeze harder".
- **Feedback fades.** Motor learning works best when feedback fades, so the meter hides itself every third rep once you're doing well ([MacIntosh 2019](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1227431/article/biofeedback-intervention-effects-for-people-with-c), [JSLHR 2023](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2022_JSLHR-22-00375)).
- **Reps and rest follow CP strength guidelines:** about 10 easy reps, rest between sets, every other day ([Verschuren 2016](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/133572), [Scholtes 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2579291)).
- **Honest where evidence is weak.** Mouth exercises are framed as control practice, never as speech treatment ([Arvedson 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1469-8749.2010.03707.x)).

<summary><b>Design decisions and sources</b></summary>

| What the research says | What we built from it |
|---|---|
| Goal-directed, task-based practice is one of the best-supported approaches in CP ([Novak 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7035308/)) | Short lessons with one clear goal each, and a daily goal in minutes |
| Games played with head movements have helped children with CP, but the studies are small ([Velasco 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5568605)) | Head steering as practice, framed honestly as a tool to use alongside therapy |
| Biofeedback helps, but the evidence is "very low" quality; feedback works best when it fades and the player chooses it ([MacIntosh 2019](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1227431/article/biofeedback-intervention-effects-for-people-with-c), [JSLHR 2023](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2022_JSLHR-22-00375)) | The live meter hides itself every third rep once a lesson goes well, and players can hide it any time |
| Evidence for mouth (oral-motor) exercises is weak ([Arvedson 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1469-8749.2010.03707.x)) | Jaw and lip units are control practice, never sold as treatment for speech or eating |
| Choice and autonomy keep people playing rehab games ([JMIR Serious Games 2024](https://games.jmir.org/2024/1/e49029)) | Nothing is locked, no lives to lose, and a weekly streak with rest days |
| Fatigue builds quickly and rest matters ([PMC4942358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4942358)) | An "I'm tired" button that earns credit, fatigue signs that stop difficulty going up, and a rest after every lesson |
| Wearable EMG biofeedback improved arm goals in a pilot study ([Bloom 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20525944/)) | Biceps lessons from two EMG sensors |
| In CP the biceps are often over-active, and games helped balance them ([EMG + VR study](https://content.iospress.com/articles/neurorehabilitation/nre1402)) | Letting go on cue counts as much as squeezing, and nothing ever says "squeeze harder" |
| Strength training in CP follows youth guidelines: sets of about 10 easy reps, rest between sets, every other day ([Verschuren 2016](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/133572), [Scholtes 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2579291)) | 10 reps per lesson, the drive between gates is the rest, and arm days are suggested every other day |
| Two-muscle control is taught step by step: each muscle alone, hold, sequence, then both ([ADAPT-MP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6050406), [UNB](https://www.unb.ca/ibme/_assets/documents/past-docs/MEC17-papers/tabor-quantifying-muscle-control.pdf), [JNER 2021](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/s12984-021-00831-5)) | The arm units go in that order: wake up, hold strong, curls, then left and right |
| Mirror movements (the other arm joining in) are common, and coping works better than drilling ([mirror movement studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3159754)) | "Just one" lets the other arm join at first and only slowly asks it to stay quiet |

Full reasoning for the arm exercises: [docs/arm-path.md](arm-path.md).
